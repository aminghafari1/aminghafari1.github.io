I’m a Bioengineering PhD candidate at the University of California, Riverside, where my research lies at the intersection of medical imaging, neuroimaging, and machine learning.

My work focuses on developing data-driven and computational methods for analyzing brain imaging data, particularly functional MRI (fMRI) and functional connectivity, with the goal of identifying robust, interpretable biomarkers of brain aging and neurodegenerative disorders. I am especially interested in connectome-based predictive modeling, dynamic functional connectivity, and individualized brain signatures for early detection and phenotyping across aging-related conditions such as Alzheimer’s disease, Parkinson’s disease, and physical frailty. I conduct my research under supervision of [Dr. Xiaoping Hu](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=OlOZit0AAAAJ&hl=en&oi=ao) from the bioengineering department of UCR, and Dr. Ilanna Bennett from the psychology department of UCR.

More broadly, my research involves medical image analysis, signal processing, and machine learning applied to high-dimensional biomedical data, with an emphasis on model interpretability, robustness, and reproducibility. I am also interested in advancing computational pipelines for multimodal imaging integration, longitudinal analysis, and personalized prediction models that can translate to clinical and real-world settings.
